SparkUp, Velocity, and many more

On stage

Eight and a half years at Opera. More than 150 conference talks.

Bruce was at Opera for eight and a half years, and in that time he gave more than 150 conference talks. Here are three: SparkUp in Poland in May 2010, with his shadow looming behind him; a leather jacket and an Opera T-shirt; and O’Reilly Velocity, in a suit, with the green mohawk.

My living room, October 2010

Chilling with Opera chums

October 2010, my house. Bruce has his socks on my coffee table, which tells you everything about the management relationship. Left to right: Vadim, now at Mozilla; Mike Taylor, now on Chrome; Bruce, now at Vivaldi; Zi Bin; and David Storey, now on Edge. Back then we all worked on one browser engine, Presto. Now three of the four run on the same engine, and it isn’t ours.

Offsite in Norway, September 2011

With the DevRel Gang

The Opera DevRel team at its biggest.

September 2011, an offsite in Norway. The Opera Developer Relations team at its largest, and where everyone went next. Chris Mills and I both did a stint at Mozilla. Bruce, pink mohawk, giving someone bunny ears, is the one whose Vivaldi label covers his Opera T-shirt. Then the evening round the fire in a hytte.

Barcelona, February 2013

A rare sight: corporate Bruce

Ready to leverage synergies…

Mobile World Congress, the most corporate week in the calendar. Bruce made an effort: a blazer, a shirt with an actual collar, the Opera lanyard. From the neck down, every inch the business development executive. From the neck up, not so much.

London, September 2025

After the schmoozing

Catch-up after a session of government schmoozing.

September 2025, London, just after one of Bruce’s government schmoozing sessions: a man who once wore a cowboy hat on stage, now briefing officials on browser competition. The mohawk is gone, but the jacket covered in pin badges is very much still there, and it’s in the web component as the “pin-jacket” top.

And now Bruce turns 60

Which got me thinking: what do you give a web standards person who has everything?
60decimal
→0x3Chexadecimal
→<ASCII
Sixty in hex is 3C. And 0x3C in ASCII is the less-than sign. The start of every HTML element.
